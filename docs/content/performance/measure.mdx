---
title: Measure a mod
description: Find a mod's slow frames and what spends them before players do.
---

Measure your mod in the game, with as many players as a real match has. The
sandbox runs your mod with one stand-in player, who does not have a hero, and it
does not hold back a slow frame. It only logs `slow: Frame took 612 ms` for an
event that runs past 500 ms. So a mod that runs well in the sandbox can still
skip most of its frames on a server.

## Play with bots

`modlock play` starts the game with your mod, and the terminal shows what the
host reports. When the mod skips frames, the terminal prints the host's
[skipped-frames line](/performance/frame-budget#frames-over-the-allowance) and,
under it, the slowest frame with the stacks the sampler caught there:

```text
! prophunt: a frame took 230 ms, 59 ms of it in host calls, and the mod skipped 2862 frames to repay slow ones
    4 of 9 samples in decode (src/net.ts:41) < readState (src/state.ts:88) < tick (src/main.ts:30) < ...
```

Each stack lists the innermost function first, up to four deep. The sampler
starts once a frame passes 8 ms, so it does not sample a frame under the
allowance.

To fill the server, have the mod add bots with `addBot` when it starts with a
test argument, such as the `probe` that `modlock play -- probe` passes. Bots
fire the same events players do, so they drive the mod's work for each player.
See
[Testing](/guides/testing#in-the-game).

## Read names, not bundle names

`modlock play` and `modlock dev` build a TypeScript or JavaScript mod without
minifying it, and they write a source map beside the bundle. The terminal maps
each sampled frame through the source map, so the stacks name your functions,
files and lines.

A published build, and a build with `--release`, is minified and does not
include a source map. Its stacks show the bundle's short names, such as
`Nc (mod.js:2)`, and so does the host's log on a server. To find where a slow
frame on a server spends its time, reproduce the frame with `modlock play`,
without `--release`.

## Time the parts of a frame

In TypeScript, [`frameClock`](/guides/toolkit#time-your-frames) times named
parts of your frame handler. Once a minute, it logs the median and slowest
frame, each part's mean and slowest time, and the parts that cost the most in
the slowest frame. Use it when the sampler shows time spread across many
functions, or to compare the cost before and after a change.

## Check before you publish

Before you publish, play a full round with a full server and watch the
terminal for these results:

- No skipped-frames line, or one that counts only a handful of frames.
- No `slow:` line.
- With `frameClock`, a slowest frame of a few milliseconds at most, since
  [the server is slower](/performance/frame-budget#leave-room-for-the-server)
  than your computer.
