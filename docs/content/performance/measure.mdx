---
title: Measure a mod
description: Find a mod's slow frames and what spends them before players do.
---

Measure in the game, with as many players as a real match. The sandbox runs
your mod with one stand-in player and no hero, and it does not hold back a slow
frame: it only logs `slow: Frame took 612 ms` for an event past 500 ms. A mod
that runs well in the sandbox can still skip most of its frames on a server.

## Play with bots

`modlock play` starts the game with your mod, and the terminal shows what the
host reports. When the mod skips frames, the terminal prints the host's
[skipped-frames line](/performance/frame-budget#frames-over-the-allowance) and,
under it, the slowest frame with the stacks the sampler caught there:

```text
! prophunt: a frame took 230 ms, 59 ms of it in host calls, and the mod skipped 2862 frames to repay slow ones
    4 of 9 samples in decode (src/net.ts:41) < readState (src/state.ts:88) < tick (src/main.ts:30) < ...
```

Each stack lists the innermost function first, up to four deep. The sampler
starts once a frame passes 8 ms, so a frame under the allowance has no samples.

To fill the server, have the mod add bots with `addBot` when it starts with a
test argument, such as the `probe` that `modlock play -- probe` passes. Bots fire the same events players do, so they drive the mod's per-player work. See
[Testing](/guides/testing#in-the-game).

## Read names, not bundle names

`modlock play` and `modlock dev` build a TypeScript or JavaScript mod without
minifying it, and write a source map beside the bundle. The terminal maps each
sampled frame through it, so stacks name your functions, files and lines.

A published build, and a build with `--release`, is minified and has no map.
Its stacks show the bundle's short names, such as `Nc (mod.js:2)`, and so does
the host's log on a server. To find what a slow frame on a server spends,
reproduce it with `modlock play` without `--release`.

## Time the parts of a frame

In TypeScript, [`frameClock`](/guides/toolkit#time-your-frames) times named
parts of your frame handler. Once a minute it logs the median and slowest
frame, each part's mean and slowest time, and the parts that cost most in the
slowest frame. Use it when the sampler shows time spread across many functions,
or to compare a change before and after.

## Check before you publish

Before you publish, play a full round with a full server and watch the
terminal:

- No skipped-frames line, or one that counts only a handful of frames.
- No `slow:` line.
- With `frameClock`, a slowest frame of a few milliseconds at most, since
  [the server is slower](/performance/frame-budget#leave-room-for-the-server)
  than your computer.
